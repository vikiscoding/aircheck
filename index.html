    How a reading works

    1. You type a place, or allow one location reading.
    2. The page asks Open-Meteo for the current weather, UV index, cloud cover and air quality.
    3. It sorts those numbers into bands and looks up a sentence written ahead of time.
    4. You see the sentence, the numbers behind it, the time of the reading and the source.

    No AI writes the sentence. Every sentence comes from a fixed table, so the same reading always gets the same words. If the table has no row, the numbers stay on the page with a note that no sentence was written for them. A reading describes now. It is not a forecast for later in the day.

    Common questions

    What does the US AQI number mean?

    The US Air Quality Index runs from 0 upward. 0–50 is Good, 51–100 is Moderate, 101–150 is Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups, 151–200 is Unhealthy, 201–300 is Very Unhealthy, and above 300 is Hazardous. The sentence treats 151 and above as very high. Open "More air readings" under a result to see PM2.5, PM10, nitrogen dioxide and ozone.

    What does "feels like" mean?

    Feels-like, or apparent, temperature is Open-Meteo's estimate of how the air feels on skin once humidity, wind and sun are counted. The page uses the same bands for both numbers: freezing under 0°C, cold under 10, cool under 18, mild under 24, warm under 30, hot under 35, and very hot from 35. A heat line appears only when the feels-like band is hotter than the air band.

    How is the burn time worked out?

    A sun line appears when the rounded UV index is 3 or higher. Moderate is 3–5, high is 6–7, very high is 8–10, and extreme is 11 or above. Choose a skin type under Sun settings to see a range. Minutes equal a published skin-type dose divided by the UV index times 1.5. The UV index already includes cloud cover. The range is an estimate for unprotected skin in direct sun, not a personal limit.

    When does the result card change colour?

    Only at the two ends, and always with a written label. Limiting conditions means a storm, heavy precipitation, very hot air or a very hot feels-like temperature, a US AQI of 151 or more, or a very high or extreme UV index. Easier conditions needs all of these at once: mild air, a mild feels-like temperature, a clear or cloudy sky, a US AQI of 50 or less, and a low UV index. Anything in between, or a missing number, keeps the card plain.

    Is there an activity line?

    One line names a single limit when a written rule is met, in this order: a storm, heavy precipitation, a US AQI of 151 or more, then heat when the feels-like band is hot or very hot and hotter than the air.

    Can I share a reading?

    After a place lookup, use "Link to this place". The link holds the place name and its Open-Meteo place id, never your coordinates. A reading from "Use my location once" has no link.

    Privacy

    No account. No history. No cookies. If you use your location, the coordinates are used once for the request and then discarded. A visit counter adds one for a browser session and skips known bots. It does not store your place, your location or your skin type. A skin type is saved on this device only if you choose Remember.
